About

Ten products, one account, no divided attention.

Exprsn started as ten separate services — a feed, a messenger, a groups product, a live-streaming service, and the identity/security plumbing underneath all of it. We consolidated them into one platform because that's how people actually want to use them: together.

From ten microservices to one platform

Each of Exprsn's modules — CA, auth, Spark, Nexus, FileVault, Vault, Timeline, Prefetch, Moderator, and Live — began life as its own standalone service, each with its own server, its own port, and its own deployment story. That made sense while each was being built and proven out on its own.

It stopped making sense once people wanted to actually use them as one product. Nobody wants a separate login for their feed, their messages, their groups, and their live streams. So we did the unglamorous work: one gateway, one Socket.IO server, one database — while keeping every module's data in its own schema, so nothing got flattened into a mess in the process.

The result runs as a single deployable unit that a small team — or a single developer — can actually operate, audit, and self-host, instead of babysitting ten separate deployments.

◆

Own your data

Self-hosted by design. Your Postgres, your Redis, your certificates — nothing routes through a third party you didn't choose.

✦

Privacy by default

Spark messages are end-to-end encrypted. Secrets and files get dedicated modules instead of shared, general-purpose storage.

◎

Real moderation

Nexus governance and Moderator's realtime queues mean community rules are enforced with actual tooling, not just good intentions.

▤

No black boxes

Plain Express, plain Sequelize, plain Socket.IO. Every module follows the same three-rule contract — nothing proprietary to reverse-engineer.
